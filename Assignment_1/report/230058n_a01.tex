    When the nearest neighbor interpolation is used, adjacent pixel values
are simply duplicated to fill the new space. This causes a highly blocky
and pixelated appearance, especially along sharp edges. In contrast,
bilinear interpolation calculates new pixel values based on a weighted
average of the four closest surrounding pixels. As a result, much
smoother transitions and a higher overall visual quality are achieved,
although this method requires slightly more computational power.

    When the standard Gaussian filter is applied, the entire image is
uniformly smoothed, which causes important structural boundaries to lose
their sharpness. In contrast, the Bilateral filter takes both spatial
distance and pixel intensity differences into account. As a result, flat
regions are effectively blurred to reduce noise, while sharp edges and
fine details are successfully protected and preserved.
